# 01 · Marketing strategy

What Bullion Trading LLC videos are for, the formats that work, how each script is built, and the claims we
can and cannot make. Source material: `public/marketing/campaign.json` (every script),
`strategy/campaign-study/bullion_knowledge/` (business brief, claim review, site archive, product catalog) and
`strategy/productions/whats-inside/` (a full brief → treatment for a story film).

## 1. Who we talk to and where

- **Primary outcome:** purchases of physical gold and silver (bars, coins, rounds); secondary: selling to the
  buyback desk, and jewelers buying casting grain (B2B).
- **Audiences used so far:** first-time buyers; New York buyers who want a physical dealer; gift buyers for
  milestones; buyers of world-famous coins/bars; price-conscious comparers; cautious buyers worried about fakes;
  owners selling gold; families marking cultural traditions; independent jewelers (casting grain).
- **Channels:** vertical social video (Reels / Shorts / TikTok, 1080×1920) and paid search/social
  (Google + Meta) — see §7.

## 2. Brand truths we may state

From owner-approved scripts (`campaign.json`) and the public site (`BUSINESS_BRIEF.txt`):

- Bullion Trading LLC, **20 West 47th Street** (Lower Level #24), Manhattan's **Diamond District**, since **2008**;
  online at **BullionTradingLLC.com**.
- Live, spot-based pricing; prices built from live quotes plus a transparent premium; reprice within minutes.
- Buyback: published prices, price lock online, ship with the provided label, paid in 1–3 business days after
  inspection; in person, a standard bar or coin typically takes 3–6 minutes from inspection to payment.
- Owner: 30+ years in precious metals.
- Closing lines: **"Buy from Bullion Trading."** (purchase films) · **"Sell it to Bullion Trading."** (seller films).

Statements that come from the owner's FAQ videos and **need owner confirmation before use as a promise in
ads**: testing gold "right in front of you" in the Manhattan office; inspecting and testing before anything
ships, with records kept. (*What's inside?* uses them — get sign-off before publishing.)

## 3. Claims we do not make

From `CLAIM_REVIEW.txt` — the short version:

1. No same-day pickup promise (possible with a verified bank check; card/PayPal can't be collected in store).
2. No same-day nationwide shipping claim (product badges say 1–3 business days).
3. No tax-free claims in ads without current jurisdiction validation.
4. No single test (magnet, ice, ring, look) presented as proof of authenticity.
5. No "lowest price", fixed premium or competitor-savings claims; premiums move during the day.
6. No investment promises: appreciation, safety, recession protection, returns.
7. Generated/illustrative scenes are labelled and never presented as the real store, staff or inventory.
8. No invented ratings, testimonials or trust figures.
9. No universal discount, easy-returns or payment-method promises.
10. Date-sensitive numbers (spot price, listing prices) are checked again on the day of publishing.

Fictional examples (listings, sellers, serials, receipts) are labelled **"fictional example"** on screen, and
counterfeit stories **never show a real refiner's product** — draw a generic bar instead.

## 4. Formats that work

| Format | Length | Structure | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Premium product film** | 20–25 s, 5 scenes | hook → choice → detail → confidence (team / pricing / place) → CTA | Your first piece, Icons of gold, A gift with meaning, A real New York connection |
| **Explainer from site content** | 22–36 s, 5–8 scenes | question or claim hook → 3–5 concrete facts, one per scene → CTA | Spot to store, Sell to us, Real or fake?, Tax by state, Traditions |
| **FAQ answer** | 40–50 s, 8–11 scenes | the customer's exact question on a card → straight answer → owner expertise → how Bullion Trading does it → CTA | the five premium-3 films |
| **B2B long form** | ~75 s, 5 movements | problem → agitation (the cost) → our offer → the 47th St advantage → CTA with address + site | The Maker's Edge |
| **Story / thriller (cloned structure)** | ~80–90 s, 10 beats | dated fact → "now picture this" → hidden consequence → "who pays? you" → the bill → escalating real evidence → it happens every day → direct question → "don't worry" replay of the same scene going right → brand | What's inside? |
| **Market brief** | 30–65 s | data HUD: thesis → drivers → sentiment/support → conclusion | BullionBrief, MetalsBrief (legacy) |

### The 10-beat story structure (from *What's inside?*)

Cloned from a reference reel's story (its analysis is in `strategy/references/story-reel/`) and re-told for
bullion. It works for any trust or protection message:

1. **Dated fact** that makes the risk possible (gold > $4,000/oz; tungsten weighs almost the same).
2. **"Now picture this."** One sympathetic, specific scenario (a bargain bar online).
3. **The consequence, made visible and timed** (scale reads 31.10 g ✓, magnet ✓ … the scan finds tungsten).
4. **Accountability turns to the viewer:** "Who takes the loss? Not the seller. They're gone. **You.**"
5. **The bill** in hard numbers (paid $3,965 → worth almost nothing).
6. **Escalation with real evidence** (the 2012 Diamond District case — sourced on screen).
7. **It happens every day** (listings multiplying, clock running).
8. **A direct question** ("Do you really know what's inside your gold?").
9. **The relief is a replay** — the *same* bar, the *same* scan, at Bullion Trading: solid gold, verified.
10. **Brand + CTA** ("From your next bar on, we've got you. Buy from Bullion Trading.").

One **carried object** (the bar's tag) travels through every scene, so the viewer follows a thing, not slides.

## 5. Writing rules for scripts

- One idea per scene; a scene is one or two short spoken sentences. Most films run 20–50 s.
- Open on the viewer's question, a concrete number or a vivid situation — never on the company name.
- Use the customer's words ("How much over spot should you really pay for gold?").
- Make numbers concrete and sourced; put the source on screen when it's external (news, prices).
- Close with the approved line (`closing` in `campaign.json`, default "Buy from Bullion Trading.").
- Write spoken numbers in words in `text` and their written form in `display`
  ("four thousand dollars" / "$4,000") so captions read naturally.
- Keep the first visual moving from frame 1 and keep reveals on the word they answer (see 03-graphics).
- Spell tricky names phonetically in `text` and correct them for captions with `caption` replacements
  (e.g. "Dhun-teh-rus" → "Dhanteras").

## 8. Cloning a reference video

When someone brings a video and says "make one like this for Bullion Trading" (the `clone-video` skill):

1. Watch the whole reference; transcribe it with word times; build time-labelled frame grids.
2. Write the **whole-piece reading** (what it makes the viewer feel/learn, the story beats, recurring systems)
   and the **timed reading** (each visual system's content, placement, entry, change, exit, tied to words).
3. Decide what to keep (story function, rhythm, the carried object, colour meaning) and what to redesign for
   bullion (subject, evidence, numbers, CTA).
4. Verify every new fact before writing it; record sources in the brief.
5. Write Brief (the goal and facts) and Treatment (the new piece, beat by beat) — see
   `strategy/productions/whats-inside/`.
