# Treatment — "What's inside?"

A quiet thriller in ten beats about one gold bar. The viewer watches a reasonable person buy a bargain bar
online, watches it pass the checks they know, and then sees it cut open: gray tungsten. The stakes land on
*you*; a real Diamond District case proves even professionals get caught; deals like it are listed every day.
Then the same bar, the same scan — at Bullion Trading's counter, gold all the way through. "From your next bar
on, we've got you."

## Look

The reference's dark HUD restraint, re-voiced for a bullion house: near-black ground with a faint dot grid and
vignette, Inter for headlines and captions, JetBrains Mono for kickers, labels, counters and source lines.

- **Gold** (#d4af37 → #f6e3a1) is the brand: caption highlight pill, the opening price, Bullion Trading's
  replay, end card. Like Blurr's purple, it is never used for the fake.
- **Red** (#e5484d) is danger and loss: stamps, struck seller, the loss counter.
- **Tungsten steel** (#8e98a4 / #4b535c) is the fake's core and anything cold.
- **Cream paper** (#efe8d8, ink #1d1b17) is documents: receipt and 2012 case file.

## The carried object

**The bar tag** (one persistent layer above the scenes, so it stays sharp through every cut): a small dark assay-style card — "FINE GOLD · 1 OZ · 999.9 · SERIAL AU-204417 · FICTIONAL
EXAMPLE". It is on the listing, rides in the parcel, sits on the scale, gets stamped TUNGSTEN CORE, hangs
under the "who" chain, is paper-clipped to the receipt, recurs in the "every day" grid, and returns in the
replay stamped VERIFIED in gold.

## Beats (narration → picture)

1. **spot** — "This October, gold is trading above four thousand dollars an ounce. And one cheap metal weighs
   almost exactly the same. Tungsten." The price card opens as the hero, centred and live from the first frame
   (ticking just under $4,000, climbing through it on "above" to $4,165, stamp ABOVE $4,000), then rises on "And"; on "weighs" two element tiles (Au 79 gold, W 74 steel) with density counters 19.32 / 19.25 and a
   balance beam that settles level; steel stamp "≈ SAME WEIGHT".
2. **picture** — "Now picture this. You find a one-ounce gold bar online, two hundred dollars under spot.
   Sealed. A serial number. A five-star seller. You tap buy." A fictional marketplace listing builds: bar art,
   price $3,965 with spot $4,165 struck, "−$200 UNDER SPOT" chip, then the three trust badges pop on their
   words (SEALED / SERIAL / ★★★★★); a big "Buy now" key-cap presses on "buy".
3. **arrives** — "It arrives. The weight checks out. A magnet won't stick. But under a thin skin of gold,
   it's tungsten." Parcel → the bar on a precision scale, mono readout counting 0.00 → 31.10 g (the
   reference's stopwatch), ✓ WEIGHT; a magnet drops and slides off, ✓ MAGNET; on "under" a scan line slices the
   bar — gold skin, steel core; red stamp **TUNGSTEN CORE**; frame tints red.
4. **who** — "So who takes the loss? Not the seller. They're gone. You." The loss is an object: a red
   −$3,965 chip, orbited by "?" marks, looking for an owner between three modern profile cards — the seller
   (gold avatar, verified tick, ★ 4.9 · 1,204 sales, TOP SELLER), the listing (thumbnail, PAID $3,965, LIVE) and
   you (avatar, "Holding the bar", an empty slot). On "Not the seller" the chip lunges at the seller and is
   refused: tick → red ✕, name struck, ACCOUNT CLOSED. On "They're gone" the profile glitches and disintegrates
   into "User not found", the listing flips to LISTING REMOVED, and your message "Is this bar real?" bounces:
   NOT DELIVERED · USER NOT FOUND. On "You." the chip drops into your card's slot, the card turns red, **You.**
   lands under the still-visible question, with a jolt and the film's biggest hit. The tungsten-stamped tag
   stays in your hands and carries on into the receipt.
5. **bill** — "You paid three thousand, nine hundred and sixty-five dollars. What you're holding is worth almost
   nothing." Cream receipt (fictional), tag clipped on; "PAID $3,965.00" types; on "worth" a red line
   "GOLD VALUE" counts down to "≈ $0" and LOSS −$3,965 in red.
6. **case** — "And it isn't just online. In 2012, a jeweler in Manhattan's Diamond District paid a hundred
   thousand dollars for four ten-ounce gold bars. Serial numbers. Paperwork. Filled with tungsten." Opens on the
   corner itself — green NYC street-sign blades W 47 St / 5 Av, red stamp SEPT 2012 — then a cream case file
   slides over them on "jeweler", already holding the four 10 oz bars (they lift and gleam on "four"); $100,000 counts up; SERIAL ✓ PAPERS ✓
   tick on their words; drill line reveals steel cores; red tape stamp **FILLED WITH TUNGSTEN**; source line
   "FOX NEWS · NBC NEWS · SEPT 19, 2012".
7. **everyday** — "Meanwhile, deals that look this good get listed every day. Quietly. With great reviews."
   Listing tiles multiply 2×2 → 4×4 → 8×8 while a date/clock runs; footer "SOLD · SOLD · SOLD · NO QUESTIONS".
8. **question** — "So, do you really know what's inside your gold?" Over the dimmed grid, large type word by
   word, **inside** in red.
9. **verify** — "Don't worry. At Bullion Trading, we never just assume a bar is genuine. We test it in front of
   you at our New York counter, and before anything ships." Kicker "SAME BAR · AT BULLION TRADING" (gold). The
   same bar and tag, the same scan line — gold all the way through; checklist ticks TESTED / IN FRONT OF YOU /
   BEFORE IT SHIPS; gold stamp **VERIFIED**.
10. **cta** — "From your next bar on, we've got you. Buy from Bullion Trading." Gold light beam, Bullion
    Trading logo, line text, pill "BullionTradingLLC.com · 20 W 47th St, New York", disclaimer.

## Sound

A low, patient pulse bed (minor, ~80 BPM feel) under beats 1–8; UI foley (key-cap presses, scale beeps,
magnet clack, parcel thud, scanner sweep, stamps, receipt printer, clock ticks accelerating in the grid); the
biggest impact on "You."; near-silence for the question; on "Don't worry" the bed resolves into warm major
pads with a soft rising shimmer into the logo.
